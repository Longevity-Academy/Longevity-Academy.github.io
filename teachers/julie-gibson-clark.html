The Story

Not a billionaire.
Just relentless.

Julie Gibson Clark is a former structural engineer turned recruiter and single mom from Phoenix, Arizona. She didn't set out to become a longevity icon — she set out to be there for her son for as long as possible.

With a disciplined routine of diet, exercise, sleep and a handful of well-chosen supplements, she slowed her biological aging to just 0.665 of a year for every calendar year — roughly 34% slower than average, effectively rolling back her biological age by more than a decade.

That result placed her #2 in the world on the Rejuvenation Olympics leaderboard — a global ranking of biological aging measured by DNA-methylation clocks — where she famously sat ahead of tech biohacker Bryan Johnson, who reportedly spends around $2 million a year chasing the same goal.
